eight ends.

The owner picked L8, the thread, and gave one instruction. In their words, "L8 but shorten the tail so the X axis matches the edge of the circle body of the g." L8's tail runs left past the bowl and its point stands 6.5 units clear of the bowl's left edge. This round holds L8's weight, a bowl of 28 with a 7 stroke, a 14 counter and a 6 dot, a tail that thins from 7 to 4 over the corner, and a 6 point. The one change is the tail's length. Each mark ends its tail where the bowl's left edge is, so the point and the bowl's edge share one vertical. What varies is how the two meet. Eight ends, with the point's centre on the edge, the point's outer edge on it, or the point tucked a unit inside, with a flat or a round underside, with a low hook that stops, with the taper spread over the short run, and with a bigger bowl as its own row. The mark is narrower than it is tall now, so the envelope is on the page as figures. Rounds one to six are folded at the end.

The pick, and the trade

G7, the wave tail. Below is the reasoning, the runner up, and a table of how much EQ each mark still says at 16 px.

G7. The wave tail, with G1 as the runner up

  • The cue is in the one part of a g that is free to move. A bowl has to stay round or the letter breaks. A stem has to stay straight. The tail is the one stroke a g lets you draw any way you like, so the curve goes there. The g stays a g and the tail becomes the plot.
  • At 16 px it is still a g, and its tail is wider and wavy. In the pixel view the ring and the stem are the same two things round one had. The tail is a 2 px line that dips and comes back. A stranger sees a g with a wave under it. A person who has seen the panel sees a cut band. That is as much as 12 px of tile can carry.
  • At 32 px and above the wave reads as a band. In the Dock and on the store tile the tail is a one band curve under the letter, which is the plot at its smallest. The wave says sound before the tagline does.
  • The one colour form is one stroke. In the menu bar it is a g with a long wavy tail. Nothing fills in and nothing goes to a dot. The site header reads as one letter next to the wordmark.
  • Runner up, G1, the peak bowl. It carries the most EQ at 128 px, since the bowl is a bell on a line and the eye reads the band first and the g second. At 16 px the bell closes to a bowl, so the cue is gone. The letter pays for it at every size. The bowl is pointed, and in the 18 px bar form it is near an A with a tail. If the owner wants the loudest statement on the store page and accepts that, G1 is the pick.
  • The one with the most cue at 16 px is G4. The light point on its tail is still a speck in the 16 px pixel view, and it is the panel's own chosen band point. It does not say EQ to a stranger. It says it to anyone who has used goui once. G4 is the pick if the toolbar matters more than the store page.
  • What is given up. The wave tail is quieter than bars or a bell. The cue is a dip of 7 units. A person who does not know EQ plots sees a flourish. G7 trades a loud cue at 128 for a cue that is still there at 32 and does not cost the letter anything at 16.
  • Calls made on this page. The first draft had the tail rise into a peak. Its top touched the bowl and the 16 px view closed into an 8, so the band is a cut, not a peak. The dip is 7 units deep and the tail runs to 8 units from the left edge. The ring is 11.5 units, a half unit under E's, and moved up and right so the tail has room. The stroke stays at 9 units. The crop for the bar form is 58 of 64, so the stroke is 9.9 units there.

The recommendation

Concept E, the letter g on an acid tile. Below it is the reasoning, then the list of what has to change to ship it.

E. The letter g, ink on acid

  • It reads at 16 px, and it is the only one that does without doubt. In the pixel view the ring, the stem and the tail are three clear things at 12 px of tile. The bars hold up too, but as four grey sticks. The bell of B reads as a hill. The ear and the waveform go to a blob.
  • It is the loudest thing in the toolbar. An acid tile with an ink letter has a clear edge on the light toolbar and the dark one. Every ink tile on this page is near the dark toolbar's grey, and the acid mark alone has to carry it there.
  • One mark, everywhere. The same g is the favicon, the Dock icon, the menu bar glyph and the mark beside the wordmark. In the site header the g next to the word goui reads as one thing, where a curve or bars next to the word read as two.
  • The mono form is a letter, which the menu bar is used to. The bars of A read fine there. The bell of B reads as a tent. The strips of C read as a menu glyph and the pills of D as a microphone.
  • What it gives up. A letter says nothing about sound. The tagline, the plot and the panel say that instead. The g here is drawn in the spirit of Atkinson Hyperlegible, not from its outline, so the wordmark and the mark are cousins, not twins.
  • Runners up. A is the cheapest change, since only the colours move, and it says equaliser at once. If the owner wants a picture of sound over a letter, A is the pick. B is the pick if the icon should be the plot, and its 16 px reading is close behind A.
  • Calls made on this page. The g is single storey, with a round bowl and a tail that hooks left under it. The stroke is 9 of 64 units, so it is 1.7 px at 12 px of tile. The tile is the accent and the letter is accent-ink, so the icon reads as a pressed accent button, which is how the panel's primary button looks.

Palette note

Every mark on this page uses the accent and the ink steps, and nothing else. The current icon is the one blue left in goui. Once it goes, the sky blue of a shelf band and the lavender of the headphone layer are the only blues, and both are audio colours, not brand colours. No concept here brings a new blue in.

  • Two polarities. An ink tile with an acid mark matches the panel. An acid tile with an ink mark reads on any toolbar and is the loudest thing in it. The recommendation takes the acid tile. If the owner wants the quieter one, E inverts cleanly, as an ink tile with an acid g, and the Dock row shows how the ink tiles of A, B, D and F look there.
  • No gradient. The current tile fades top to bottom. A flat tile matches the token set, which is flat ink in five steps, and a flat tile is one colour test in the icon script instead of a mix.
  • The menu bar takes no colour. Only alpha counts there. The mono symbols are drawn in currentColor so this page can show them black and white, and the script writes black.
