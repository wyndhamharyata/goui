The letter g,
with a graph for a tail.

The owner gave a direction. Merge H2 and H4, so one mark holds the band point in the bowl and ends its tail in the point. Then, since the tail comes off the stem at an angle the way a graph line does, draw its curve as a graph line and not as a hook, and do not fix on a parabola. A graph can be many things. This round keeps round three's letterform, a circle bowl with the stem as its right side carried down, at a stroke of 7 units. The bowl moves right and the tail runs out past it, so the letter fills the safe box more evenly on both axes. The eight marks differ in the tail alone. Each tail is a short list of path commands, printed under its mark with its anchors and control points drawn, so the curve is on the record. Rounds one to three are folded at the end.

01

Eight tails

One letterform, eight tails. The bowl is a circle 28 units across with a counter of 14, and a dot of 6 units stands in the counter, so the bowl is the band handle the panel draws. The stem is the bowl's right side carried straight down. The tail runs out of the stem, under the bowl and past its left edge, and ends in the chosen band's point, 10.5 units across with a light centre. Every stroke is 7 units. Nothing is traced from a typeface. A g's tail is read as a descender, from the stem down and out to the left. A graph line is read from left to right. Each tail below has to be both at once, and the test is two questions. Is it still the tail of a g? Is it a graph line, and not a hook or a bell? Not one of the eight is a symmetric peak. The tails are a straight slope, a dip and a rise, a shelf and a drop, a peak off centre, a shallow wide bell, a line with one step, a line that bends late, and a gentle S.

02

Safe area, balance and the curve

The safe box is 8 to 56, the dashed square. The thin pink box is the ink's bounding box and the four numbers are its margins to the safe box, in grid units. The cross is the tile's centre and the pink dot is the ink's centre of mass. The lavender dots are the tail's anchors and the lavender squares are its control points, each joined to its anchor, and the path commands are printed under the table. Round three's letter was 32.5 wide and 44 tall, with side margins of 6.75 and 8.75 against 1.25 and 2.75 above and below. This round a mark passes when it is inside the safe box, every margin is at least one unit, the four margins are within 3 units of each other, and the box centre is within 1.5 units of the middle. The ink centre is printed and not judged. A g's bowl and stem are on its right and its tail is one stroke on its left, so the ink centre of this letter is right of its box centre, and the box is what the eye reads against the tile's corners. Bowl clearance is the least clear tile between the tail and the bowl. It has to be at least 3 units so the clear band under the bowl stays open at 32 px.

03

At the real sizes

The tile in the 16 px icon is 12 px wide, so a stroke of 7 units is 1.3 px, the bowl is 5.3 px across, the whole letter is about 8 px wide and the point is 2 px. The pixel view on the right is the test. Is it a g and not a 9? A 9's tail never runs past the left of its bowl, so look for the tail past the bowl. Then look for anything in it a plain g does not have.

06

The pick, and what each tail costs

J3, the shelf and the drop, with J6, the step, as the runner up. Below is the reasoning and a table of what each tail costs.

J3. The shelf and the drop, with J6 as the runner up

  • It is a g first. The tail comes off the stem, runs level under the bowl the way H2's did, and turns out past the bowl's left edge to the point. The step down is in the part of the tail that is already past the bowl, so the letter's own corner and its clear band under the bowl are unchanged. At every size the first reading is g.
  • The tail is a band the panel draws. Read left to right it is a high shelf, one of the two band types in goui, drawn the way the graph draws it, with a soft transition over ten units and a level run on each side. It is not a peak and not a hook.
  • The drop reads at 32 px. On the store tile and in the Dock the tail has a visible kink, and next to the tile's own plot it reads as a piece of the same line. At 16 px the drop goes and the tail is a 2 px line with a lump at its end, which is the same for all eight.
  • Balance and the safe box. Section 02 has the numbers. The ink is 11 to 53 across and 10 to 54.7 down, so the letter is 42 wide and 44.7 tall. The margins are 3 left, 3 right, 2 top and 1.3 bottom. Round three's were 6.75, 8.75, 1.25 and 2.75. The box centre is on the middle across and 0.4 below it. The ink centre is 4.7 right of the middle, which is the letter's bowl and stem on the right. The least clear tile between the tail and the bowl is 3.5 units.
  • The 16 px reading, plainly. The pixel view shows a bold ring with its counter closed, a tail that runs 4 px past the bowl's left edge, and a lump at the tail's end. It reads as a g and not a 9, since a 9's tail never clears the left of its bowl. The dot in the counter is gone at 16 px. It is there at 32 px as a dark centre in the ring, and it is clear from 48 px up.
  • Runner up, J6, the step. The one tail that still says graph in the menu bar at 18 px, where the notch is visible. At 128 px it is the most mechanical of the eight, a trace on a circuit board more than a curve, and a hard step is a thing the panel's curve never draws. It is the pick if the toolbar and menu bar matter more than the store page.
  • J7 is the quiet fallback. The late bend is H2 with the point dropped. It keeps the most of round three and says the least about graphs. If J3's step reads as a pipe to the owner, J7 is the safe move.
  • Calls made on this page. The stroke is 7 of 64, a half unit under round three, so the point and the counter dot both have room. The bowl moved 6.75 units right and the tail runs 13 units past its left edge, so the letter is 42 wide against round three's 32.5. The corner at the stem is 4 units on the three level tails and a cubic on the rest. Every tail ends at x 16.25 and the point's height is the one thing that moves with the curve.

The owner kept the idea of the letter g and turned down the way rounds one and two drew it. Three faults were named. The letter was a font glyph with things put in it. The marks were off centre and lopsided. The marks ran out to the tile edge. Two seeds stayed, the bars in the counter of G2 and the point on the tail of G4. 07

Six marks

One letterform, six ideas. The bowl is a circle 30 units across with a counter of 15. The stem is the bowl's right side carried straight down. The tail turns on a corner of 4.5 units, tight enough to read as an L, and runs flat to 2.5 units past the bowl's left edge. Every stroke is 7.5 units. Nothing is traced from a typeface. Three drafts came before this one and are not shown. A wide rounded bowl with a soft corner read as the numeral 9 at every size, and a small bowl with a long sweeping tail read as a 9 or an Arabic waw. The tight corner is what makes this a g. The first three marks come straight from the two seeds. The last three move the cue into the counter in a different way each time.

08

Safe area and balance

The store tile keeps 16 px clear in 128, which is 8 units of 64, so the safe box is 8 to 56. The dashed box below is that safe box. Each mark is drawn in the accent on the 64 unit grid, and this page rasterises the glyph at ten samples per unit and reads the ink back. The thin box is the ink's bounding box. The four numbers are the margins from the ink to the safe box in grid units. The cross is the tile's centre and the dot is the ink's centre of mass, which is the optical centre. A g's bowl and stem are on its right and its tail is thin and on its left, so the ink centre of any g is right of its box centre. Each mark is placed where the two disagree by the same amount, one unit each side of the axis. A mark passes when every margin is at least one unit, both centres are within a unit of the middle, and the ink centre is not below it. A g is taller than it is wide, so the side margins are larger than the top and bottom ones. That is the letter, not an error.

The pick, and the runner up

H2, the point on the tail, with H1 as the runner up. Below is the reasoning and a table of how each mark does on the three tests.

H2. The point on the tail, with H1 as the runner up

  • The cue is the one thing that still shows at 16 px. The point is 10.5 units across, so it is 2 px in the toolbar icon and 8.5 px in the Dock. Bars in a counter of 15 units are a smudge at 32 px and gone at 16. A dot on a tail is not.
  • It is a letter first. A ball at the end of a tail is a thing letters do, so a stranger reads g and does not see a thing stuck on. A person who has dragged a band in the panel sees the handle.
  • It is balanced and inside the box. Section 02 has the numbers. The ink is 14.75 to 47.25 across and 9.25 to 53.25 down. The margins are 6.75 left, 8.75 right, 1.25 top and 2.75 bottom. The box centre is one unit left of the middle and the ink centre one unit right of it, which is the split the section describes. The ink centre is a quarter unit above the middle.
  • Runner up, H1, the plot in the bowl. Three bars standing on the counter's floor is the clean reading of the seed the owner liked best. It is the strongest mark at 128 px and in the store tile. At 16 px the counter closes and it is a plain g. It is the pick if the store page matters more than the toolbar.
  • H3 is both, and it is one thing too many. On the tile it is the richest mark. In the pixel view it is H2. In the menu bar the bars fill in and it is H2 again. The second cue costs the clean bowl and buys nothing below 48 px.
  • H5 and H6 fail the first test. A solid bowl with a hole cut in it is a disc with a hook, not a letter. At 128 px a stranger sees a face or a mushroom before a g. At 16 px both are a blob with a tail. They are on the page as the record of the idea.
  • What the letterform changed. The stem is not a stroke laid over a ring. It is the ring's right side carried down, so the outside of the letter is one line from the top of the bowl to the corner. The corner is tight, which is the one thing that separates a geometric g from a 9. The tail is flat and ends with a round cap or the point. The whole letter is 32.5 units wide and 43 tall. In rounds one and two the letter was 51 tall and ran to the tile's edge.
  • Calls made on this page. The stroke is 7.5 of 64, down from 9, so the bowl keeps 5 units of clear tile between its bottom and the tail. The point is centred on the tail's line and its clearance to the bowl is more than 5. The light centre of the point is 4.6 units across. The one colour crop is the safe box itself, so the glyph fills 68 percent of the bar icon's width.

The pick, and the trade

G7, the wave tail. Below is the reasoning, the runner up, and a table of how much EQ each mark still says at 16 px.

G7. The wave tail, with G1 as the runner up

  • The cue is in the one part of a g that is free to move. A bowl has to stay round or the letter breaks. A stem has to stay straight. The tail is the one stroke a g lets you draw any way you like, so the curve goes there. The g stays a g and the tail becomes the plot.
  • At 16 px it is still a g, and its tail is wider and wavy. In the pixel view the ring and the stem are the same two things round one had. The tail is a 2 px line that dips and comes back. A stranger sees a g with a wave under it. A person who has seen the panel sees a cut band. That is as much as 12 px of tile can carry.
  • At 32 px and above the wave reads as a band. In the Dock and on the store tile the tail is a one band curve under the letter, which is the plot at its smallest. The wave says sound before the tagline does.
  • The one colour form is one stroke. In the menu bar it is a g with a long wavy tail. Nothing fills in and nothing goes to a dot. The site header reads as one letter next to the wordmark.
  • Runner up, G1, the peak bowl. It carries the most EQ at 128 px, since the bowl is a bell on a line and the eye reads the band first and the g second. At 16 px the bell closes to a bowl, so the cue is gone. The letter pays for it at every size. The bowl is pointed, and in the 18 px bar form it is near an A with a tail. If the owner wants the loudest statement on the store page and accepts that, G1 is the pick.
  • The one with the most cue at 16 px is G4. The light point on its tail is still a speck in the 16 px pixel view, and it is the panel's own chosen band point. It does not say EQ to a stranger. It says it to anyone who has used goui once. G4 is the pick if the toolbar matters more than the store page.
  • What is given up. The wave tail is quieter than bars or a bell. The cue is a dip of 7 units. A person who does not know EQ plots sees a flourish. G7 trades a loud cue at 128 for a cue that is still there at 32 and does not cost the letter anything at 16.
  • Calls made on this page. The first draft had the tail rise into a peak. Its top touched the bowl and the 16 px view closed into an 8, so the band is a cut, not a peak. The dip is 7 units deep and the tail runs to 8 units from the left edge. The ring is 11.5 units, a half unit under E's, and moved up and right so the tail has room. The stroke stays at 9 units. The crop for the bar form is 58 of 64, so the stroke is 9.9 units there.

The recommendation

Concept E, the letter g on an acid tile. Below it is the reasoning, then the list of what has to change to ship it.

E. The letter g, ink on acid

  • It reads at 16 px, and it is the only one that does without doubt. In the pixel view the ring, the stem and the tail are three clear things at 12 px of tile. The bars hold up too, but as four grey sticks. The bell of B reads as a hill. The ear and the waveform go to a blob.
  • It is the loudest thing in the toolbar. An acid tile with an ink letter has a clear edge on the light toolbar and the dark one. Every ink tile on this page is near the dark toolbar's grey, and the acid mark alone has to carry it there.
  • One mark, everywhere. The same g is the favicon, the Dock icon, the menu bar glyph and the mark beside the wordmark. In the site header the g next to the word goui reads as one thing, where a curve or bars next to the word read as two.
  • The mono form is a letter, which the menu bar is used to. The bars of A read fine there. The bell of B reads as a tent. The strips of C read as a menu glyph and the pills of D as a microphone.
  • What it gives up. A letter says nothing about sound. The tagline, the plot and the panel say that instead. The g here is drawn in the spirit of Atkinson Hyperlegible, not from its outline, so the wordmark and the mark are cousins, not twins.
  • Runners up. A is the cheapest change, since only the colours move, and it says equaliser at once. If the owner wants a picture of sound over a letter, A is the pick. B is the pick if the icon should be the plot, and its 16 px reading is close behind A.
  • Calls made on this page. The g is single storey, with a round bowl and a tail that hooks left under it. The stroke is 9 of 64 units, so it is 1.7 px at 12 px of tile. The tile is the accent and the letter is accent-ink, so the icon reads as a pressed accent button, which is how the panel's primary button looks.

Palette note

Every mark on this page uses the accent and the ink steps, and nothing else. The current icon is the one blue left in goui. Once it goes, the sky blue of a shelf band and the lavender of the headphone layer are the only blues, and both are audio colours, not brand colours. No concept here brings a new blue in.
