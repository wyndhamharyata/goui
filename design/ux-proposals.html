goui design · one system

One set of parts.
Every goui screen.

This page redesigns every goui screen as one system. Section 01 holds the tokens and the accent picker. Section 02 holds twelve primitives. Sections 03 and 04 build the panel, the guide, the Mac menu, the store tile and the site only from those. Section 05 maps today's classes to the primitives, for the code pass. Every frame is live and plays one shared sound, so an edit in the narrow panel reaches the wide panel, the Mac menu, the tile and the site. The curve uses the RBJ cookbook math. The spectrum and the level are fake.

02

Primitives

Twelve, each shown once with its variants. Each card is live. Today the panel has seven row layouts, four heading styles, ten de facto buttons, two tab controllers and two segmented controls. Here it has one of each.

Layout is four helpers, not components: ui-stack, ui-cluster, ui-split and ui-grid. Two text styles, ui-text-hint and ui-text-fine, carry hints and credits. Every colour comes from a tone class that sets --tone.

Try it

  • Hold A/B. The curve goes grey and dotted, the title line says the EQ is off, and a release anywhere brings it back. It never stays down.
  • Tune. Drag a point. It moves after 5 px, and Shift gives fine steps. Pull a peak's square handles, or scroll on a point, for Q. Double click empty space for a new band.
  • Pick Low shelf in the band type. The Q stepper goes away, and the line under the fields says a shelf has no Q.
  • Library, Headphone. Type hd 6. Each result names its source, form, rig and target, and AutoEQ's pick is marked.
  • Library, My ear. Paste is filled with a sample here, so Apply works on its own. The real box starts empty.

What changed from today

  • No cards. An eyebrow and a hairline name each group. Listen is about 60 px shorter.
  • The chain. The three layers stack flush, each with a bar in its colour. A layer you have not added keeps its place as an empty row with its add button, so the order never changes.
  • dB sliders fill from 0 dB, so a cut and a boost read apart at a glance.
  • The band type is a 3 by 2 grid. More is the sixth cell and opens a select of the rare types.
  • The inspector shows only the fields a type uses. Today a shelf shows its Q greyed out. Here it shows none, with the reason under the fields.
  • One list row. Presets, AutoEQ results, starter sounds, Sets and the table rows are one primitive.

The places a screen asked for more, and the call made each time.

Three tabs, three layers Built

Listen for everyday use, Tune for the graph, Library for everything you keep. The sound is a headphone layer, your ear and your taste. This is the panel as it ships today.

Number table

The panel as it ships

  • Three layers. Headphone, My ear and Taste each have a row on Listen with its own switch, source and name, and Change. The headphone row plays flat with ×, and the ear and taste rows come off.
  • Library tabs. Each layer has its own tab and its own preset list, with Save, Save as new, Import, Paste and Export. The headphone tab holds the AutoEQ search.
  • Listen. The layer stack, the live graph and the Bass, Vocals and Treble sliders with the Auto preamp and Level match.
  • Tune. Every band of every layer. Ear bands carry a gold ring and Taste bands a pink one, and each layer draws its own dashed curve under the total.
  • Whole sound. Export writes all three layers and the sliders as one AutoEQ file.
  • Wide. From 900 px, Listen, Tune and Library show as three columns.
  • Preamp. Auto follows the largest boost, so the sound never clips. Moving the slider sets it by hand. The light by the level turns red when the EQ can clip.
  • Header. The title of the tab goui hears, or what to click when it hears nothing, and the level with the clip light.
G

Sets Proposed

One press brings back a headphone, your ear and your taste together. A Set points at three presets and the sliders.

How it works

  • What a Set holds. One headphone preset, one ear preset and one taste preset, plus Bass, Vocals and Treble. It points at the presets, so an edit to a preset reaches every Set that uses it.
  • One press. A Set applies all three layers and the sliders, so the sound is exactly what the Set holds. An empty ear or taste slot takes that layer off, and an empty headphone slot plays Flat.
  • Listen. One picker row. It names the Set that plays now and switches on a pick, and it stays one line however many Sets you keep.
  • Library. A fourth tab, Sets, with the plain list of names. Each row names the three presets, and Save as Set is above it.
  • Saving. Save as Set needs each layer with bands saved as a preset first. When a layer still has unsaved bands, an alert names it and stops.
  • A deleted preset. The Set marks that slot as missing and treats it as empty, so the press still works.
  • Wide. The picker row is taken off Listen from 900 px, so with Listen and Library side by side, the Sets tab is the one place they appear.

Later, not in this release

  • Device rule. A Set remembers an output device and applies when the Mac switches to it. Unsaved edits at that moment need a rule of their own.

Combine: AutoEQ for neutral, eqbyear for your ear

The goal is two steps. AutoEQ gives a neutral baseline for your headphones. An eqbyear test then tunes that baseline to your own hearing. Combine puts both into the sound you hear.

The math is exact

  • Bands in a chain multiply. In dB they add. Putting the eqbyear bands after the AutoEQ bands gives exactly AutoEQ plus eqbyear. Nothing is fitted or rounded.
  • The order in the chain does not change the sound. It only changes the band numbers.
  • Your sample, a -5 dB peak at 94 Hz and a -3 dB peak at 7,732 Hz, becomes bands 7 and 8 after the six AKG bands.
  • The band count is no concern. Web Audio runs dozens of bands with no trouble.

The preamp is the one number that does not add

  • Each file's preamp only covers its own boosts. Two cuts need no preamp at all. Two boosts at one frequency need more than both preamps together.
  • With Auto on, goui sets the preamp from the combined curve and ignores the preamp line of each file.
  • With Auto off, the preamp starts at the headphone file's value, and the clip light warns you.
  • Export writes one flat AutoEQ file: the headphone bands, then the ear bands, then one preamp for the total.

Run the eqbyear test with AutoEQ on

The eqbyear result is a correction to whatever you heard during the test. Run the test in a tab that goui captures, with the AutoEQ baseline on. The result is then a correction on top of AutoEQ. A test run with no EQ also measures the headphone's own color. AutoEQ fixes that color too, so combining the two would fix it twice.

E

Add on top

Paste or import a file, then pick Add on top. Its bands join the end of the list you have.

Press Add on top. The mock then opens Tune.

How it works

  • For one quick merge, with nothing new to learn.
  • Paste and Import show two buttons: Replace, as today, and Add on top.
  • Library picks get a small + next to each result, for the same Add on top.
  • After the merge the sound is one list of bands. The card reads "AKG N700NC M2 + Ear test, edited".
  • Save keeps the merged list as one saved sound.
  • Cost. The ear part has no switch of its own, so you cannot hear AutoEQ alone for a compare. A new headphone replaces both parts. To get your ear tuning back, you add the file again.
F

Layers Chosen

The sound is a headphone layer and an ear layer, each with its own switch. The sliders apply on top of both. This is the design we built.

A pick from the search goes into the layer chosen above. A headphone pick goes into the headphone layer unless you choose My ear.

How it works

  • For your workflow: AutoEQ for a neutral base, eqbyear for your ear on top.
  • Listen shows one row per layer. Each row has a switch, the source and the name, and Change. Turn My ear off to hear the AutoEQ baseline alone.
  • No ear layer yet? The card shows "Add your ear tuning on top". It opens the Library with My ear chosen.
  • A new headphone changes the headphone layer only. Your ear layer stays.
  • Tune shows every band. Ear bands have a gold ring and come after a "My ear" label in the chip row. The graph draws each layer's own curve dashed, and the total solid.
  • Library. Paste and Import ask which layer the file is for.
  • Save keeps both layers. Export writes one flat AutoEQ file for other apps.
  • Cost. One new idea to learn, the layer. The stored config gets a layer per band, so older configs need a read path.

Changed after the proposal

  • Two lists. The Library has one section per layer. Each holds its own presets and its own Save, Import, Paste and Export. The Put into pair is gone.
  • eqbyear button. The button is in the My ear section. It turns My ear off and opens eqbyear.com in a new tab. No new permission is needed, since opening a tab needs none.
  • Save. An AutoEQ pick fills in the headphone name, so Save keeps it in one press. After an edit the button reads Save changes. Save as new keeps a second preset.
  • Preset lists. The preset that plays now is marked. A pick plays it again. The × deletes it. A sound saved before the two lists splits into both lists.
  • New band. A double click joins the layer of the selected band, or the headphone layer. Each chip row has its own Add band chip.

Decision

F. Your goal is two separate steps, and F keeps them separate. AutoEQ alone compares against AutoEQ plus your ear with one switch, and another headphone keeps your ear tuning.

A

Simple first

Pick a sound, nudge three sliders, done. The graph stays small until you ask for it.

How it works

  • For someone who wants their headphone fix and a little more bass.
  • First screen. The sound you use, a small curve, three sliders and nothing else.
  • Q on the graph. Fine tune opens the same graph as design B, with side handles on the selected peak.
  • Narrow width. The sliders need no exact dragging. The graph grows only when you open Fine tune.
  • One place. The search box finds your saved sounds and the AutoEQ headphones in one list. Change opens it in place.
  • Grows to. On a wide panel, Fine tune shows the graph and the inspector side by side.
  • Cost. The three sliders are extra bands on top of the profile. An expert may want them as normal bands they can see.
B

Graph and inspector

The graph stays in front. Tap a point and one inspector opens for that band only.

How it works

  • For someone who tunes by ear and wants the graph in front.
  • First screen. The graph, the band chips and one inspector for the band you picked.
  • Q on the graph. Pull the side handles, scroll on a point, or press the Q buttons in the inspector.
  • Narrow width. The zoom pills stretch Lows, Mids or Highs to the full width. Tall turns the graph so frequency runs down the panel. Shift gives fine steps. The steppers move one semitone, 0.5 dB or about 12% of Q.
  • One place. Library opens as a sheet with search, save, import, paste and export. The rows of five boxes become chips plus one inspector. The full table is one tap away.
  • Grows to. The graph, the inspector and the table side by side.
  • Cost. A newcomer still meets a graph first.
D

Tall graph

Use the long side of the panel for frequency. Low notes at the top, high notes at the bottom.

How it works

  • For an experiment that gives frequency the tall side of the panel.
  • First screen. A tall graph, from 20 Hz at the top to 20 kHz at the bottom, with gain left to right. This follows your rotated view.
  • Q on the graph. The side handles run up and down.
  • Narrow width. Frequency gets about 440 px instead of about 320 px. Gain needs less room and fits the width.
  • One place. Library and Table open as sheets from the bottom bar.
  • Grows to. On a wide panel it turns back to the usual left to right graph.
  • Cost. Every other EQ tool draws frequency left to right. There is no simple mode.
