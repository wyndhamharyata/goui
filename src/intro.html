goui

A parametric EQ for the audio of a browser tab. Start from an AutoEQ profile for your headphone. Then tune the sound to your own ear.

What it is for

  • goui runs the audio of one browser tab through an equalizer. You then hear that tab through your own headphones.
  • The first step is an AutoEQ profile for your headphone. That profile makes the headphone sound neutral.
  • The second step is your own ear. An eqbyear test measures what your ear still wants. goui keeps that correction apart from the headphone profile. You can then hear each one alone.
  • goui changes the sound inside the browser. It cannot change other apps. For the whole computer, use a tool such as SoundSource.
  • Your presets stay on this computer. The only download is the AutoEQ headphone list from GitHub.

Step by step

  1. Pin goui. Click the Extensions icon in the toolbar. It looks like a puzzle piece. Press the pin beside goui. The goui icon then stays in the toolbar.
  2. Open a tab that plays audio. Music, a video, a radio stream.
  3. Click the goui icon on that tab. The side panel opens. goui starts hearing that tab. The icon shows a green ON badge.
  4. Pick your headphone. In the Library, under 1 Headphone, type your model. Pick it from the AutoEQ list. Press Save to keep it as your preset.
  5. Measure your ear. This step is optional. Under 2 My ear, press Measure my ear with eqbyear. Run the test in the tab that opens. Paste the result back. Press Save to keep it.
  6. Fine-tune by hand. In Tune, drag a point to move a band. In Listen, the three sliders change the bass, the vocals and the treble.
  7. Turn goui off. Click the goui icon on that tab again. Closing the tab also turns goui off.
